# Basics

1. **Revenue management** :  is the application of disciplined analytics that predict consumer behaviour at the micro‐market levels and optimize product availability and price to maximize revenue growth.
2. **Aim** : The primary aim of revenue management is selling the right product to the right customer at the right time for the right price and with the right pack.
3. to increase top line revenue and bottom line profitability in any service industry possessing the following characteristics:
  1. Demand for the service can be divided into clear market segments and sensitivity to prices varies among the market segments.
  2. The firm’s capacity is relatively fixed; it is expensive or impractical to add or subtract inventory in the short run, though there may be
    some ability to shift it.
  3. There is a time dimension to the provision of the service – once that time has passed, the inventory loses all of its value.
  4. The cost of selling an additional unit of the existing capacity is low relative to the price of the service.
  5. There is an opportunity to evaluate and accept or reject order requests in advance of the performance of the service,
    Or
  6. There is considerable flexibility to adjust prices quickly to reflect variations in the balance of supply and demand.
  7. There are definite peaks and valleys in demand, which can be predicted, but not with a high degree of certainity
4. A company can utilize these different categories like a series of levers in the sense that all are usually available, but only one or two may drive revenue in a given situation.

The primary levers are:

1. Pricing : 
1. anticipating the value created for customers and then setting specific prices to capture that value.
2. A company may decide to price against their competitors or even their own products, but the most value comes from pricing strategies that closely follow market conditions and demand especially at a segment level.
2. Inventory : how we manage capacity and provide it to customer, it the item like membership?, how to keep the demand and revenue up
3. Marketing : market so that demand goes up, temorary discounted prices
4. Channels : different channels, different price and discount, promotions

#### Pricing

practice of defining selling price, depends upon

1. ease of calculation
2. estimation and evaluaiton of cost
3. market competative analysis

Pricing can be done based on following things

1. demand : higher price for higher demand item
2. cost : high price for product which cost high to produce or buy
3. market penetration : lower price to enter in a market as a competitor
4. price skimming : high price for new tech, inovation whihc can get high demand
5. package/bundle pricing : combining price of multiple items keeping it less then buying individual, so that other tms can be sold too
6. optimum product price : base price + additional price if addons needed (many options for customers)
7. physchological : pricing bsed on phychology like setting price 99 instead of 100 cause 99 seems resonably less than 100
8. promotional price : less price in promotion for less period
9. captive profuct pricing : sell base at lower price and other needed part on high, like printers and ink, movie ticket and popcorn
